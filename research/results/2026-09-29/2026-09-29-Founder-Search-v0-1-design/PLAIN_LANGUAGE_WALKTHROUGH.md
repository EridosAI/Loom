# Founder Search v0.1 — what would happen

**This is a proposal for review, not a run.**

One bug starts with ordinary unlearned P in the current world. It has P's built-in anatomy and spontaneous activity, but no supplied navigation skill, source map or experience borrowed from another bug. Its body, orientation and mover phase come from the existing birth rules. Its chemical surroundings have the ordinary lawful history for that phase.

The bug acts through P itself. It does not use the B1 steering controller, and nobody tells it which way to turn. Movement, contact, energy use, damage, intake and repair have their existing consequences. All of P's normal plasticity operates. A lucky encounter counts as real experience; it is not disqualified because it happened by chance.

Following Jason's timing decision, we initially observe it for up to **600 seconds of its own time**, or until it dies. We preserve everything, including failed exploration, harmful contact, doing very little and never finding a source. At the observation boundary we save the complete bug and world. A pause does not age either of them.

We ask three separate questions of its record: **What happened? What changed internally? Did that change appear to matter usefully later?** Finding energy once only answers the first. Changed weights only answer part of the second. A later useful response, connected to that experience and to a pathway actually being used, is more interesting—but we still record other possible explanations.

The proposed population is **12 such blank starts**, with the same ordinary anatomy and separately addressed life histories. We review all twelve before selecting anything. This is a search for promising development, not a requirement that all bugs succeed or a contest scored by survival.

After review and separate execution approval, we could continue up to four bugs to a total age of **900 seconds**—another 300 seconds of experience. Up to three places would follow specific developmental questions; one would go to an otherwise eligible bug chosen independently of success. That helps keep early lucky source contact from deciding everything. A further review could propose up to two continuations to **1,800 seconds**—another 900 seconds. Jason has selected those age checkpoints; which individuals continue and the proposed allocation of places remain review decisions. No extension is automatically executable.

A continuing bug keeps its actual learned structure, memories, transient state, injuries, reserves, world state and random-stream position. We do not rewind, clean it up, refill its food or put it somewhere better. Our decision to watch it longer is invisible to it.

If a bug's own experience appears to produce persistent useful change, it can be called a **founder candidate**. That is an invitation to inspect more closely, not proof of general intelligence or a declaration of Founder-0. Choosing a founder, making branches or exporting an “instinct-only” version would require later decisions. This design does none of those things.

If none develops, we examine why. Did they scarcely encounter anything consequential? Did they encounter things but fail to retain or use the experience? Did the observation window or recorder stop us too early? A smaller nursery is only one possible later hypothesis; zero founders does not automatically justify it.

This is a substantial local computation. The provisional capacity allowance is about **57 machine-hours and 200 GB for the first twelve windows and their review**, or **82 machine-hours and 280 GB** if both continuation stages are later approved. These figures include preparation/analysis allowances and evidence workspace; they are not measured long-run P performance. Recorder scaling and exact continuation support need narrow engineering work before any launch packet.

**The decision now is whether this is the right first search design. No bugs were run, no world was changed and no execution permission was created.**
