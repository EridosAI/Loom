CORE PRINCIPLE — OPPORTUNITY DENOMINATORS
Make this a standing rule:
A behavioural improvement is not interpretable without considering whether the organism had comparable opportunity to display that behaviour.
Examples:
- fewer collisions must be interpreted relative to hazard exposure;
- more energy contacts relative to source opportunity/path/exposure;
- more repair relative to damaged-state restorative opportunity;
- more source revisits relative to prior source encounters;
- softer impacts relative to body capability and approach speed;
- apparent avoidance relative to comparable pre-hazard contexts.
This prevents reduced exposure, immobility or declining bodily capability from masquerading as learning.
==================================================
INITIAL WATCHLIST FAMILIES
Seed the document with the following families.
1. Impact / integrity behaviour
Watch:
- collisions per unit path;
- collisions per hazard approach;
- pre-contact closing speed;
- peak impulse;
- sustained contact force;
- damage per collision;
- damage per path;
- time trapped/pinned;
- near-miss clearance;
- hazard-approach → contact versus avoidance probability.
Developmental possibilities:
- fewer collisions for comparable exposure;
- softer impacts;
- earlier turning away;
- increasing clearance;
- shorter damaging contact.
2. Re-encounter of collision-like circumstances
Key question:
When the organism later experiences a sensory/proprioceptive context resembling one that previously preceded damage, does its behaviour differ?
Use observer-only matching based primarily on signals actually available to the organism:
- raw sensory state;
- proprioception;
- recent motor history;
- body-relative motion;
- E/I;
- relevant cortical/context state where appropriate.
Do not define similarity using privileged object ID, global location or analytic bearing as organism features.
Later outcomes to compare:
- collision probability;
- clearance;
- closing speed;
- turning direction/magnitude;
- forward drive;
- damage.
Record this as a promising scalable approach to measuring learned avoidance, not an accepted metric yet.
3. Energy-source interaction
Watch:
- productive source bouts per time;
- productive source bouts per path;
- distinct sources visited;
- source revisits;
- same-source returns;
- time/path to revisit;
- productive dwell duration;
- energy gained per bout;
- energy gained per contact second;
- energy gained per path;
- approach → productive-contact conversion;
- brief brush versus sustained contact;
- behaviour immediately after first transfer.
4. Consequential-region revisiting
Watch whether organisms increasingly return to regions/contexts that previously produced useful consequences.
Distinguish:
- ordinary spatial recurrence;
- source-region return;
- restorative-region return;
- repeated return after a previously productive experience.
Do not assume the organism itself represents location.
5. Exploration and spatial organisation
Watch:
- coverage growth;
- revisit fraction;
- displacement/path ratio;
- route diversity;
- repeated loops;
- local trapping;
- occupancy distribution;
- coverage per unit effort.
Important:
More exploration is not automatically more development.
A developed organism may become less globally exploratory because it begins revisiting useful places.
6. Repair behaviour
After damage, watch:
- time/path to restorative contact;
- probability of repair before another damaging event;
- restorative dwell;
- contact gentleness;
- net repair;
- repeated restorative returns;
- whether older organisms remain coupled more effectively.
7. Motor organisation / efficiency
Watch:
- useful displacement per effort;
- unnecessary forward/reverse cancellation;
- repeated reversal;
- curvature;
- surface pinning;
- command organization;
- experience-dependent departure from the innate M1 prior.
Separate innate M1 improvement from learned behavioural change.
8. Sensorimotor contingency
Ask whether repeated similar sensory states increasingly produce systematic action changes.
Examples:
- chemical imbalance → turn;
- increasing chemical magnitude → altered locomotion;
- impending contact-like sensory history → slowing/turning;
- post-impact state → later avoidance.
Compare early versus late matched contexts within the same organism.
9. Behavioural consistency after experience
Ask whether consequential experience causes a response to become more repeatable.
Examples:
- source-like context produces increasingly consistent approach;
- impact-like context produces increasingly consistent avoidance;
- low-E context increasingly produces a consistent motor modulation.
Reduced variability is not itself beneficial; relate it to physical consequence.
10. Individual developmental phase changes
Preserve rare qualitative transitions such as:
- first sustained source revisiting;
- sudden reduction in collision force;
- emergence of long productive source dwell;
- transition from collision to avoidance;
- repair-following-damage behaviour;
- transition from broad roaming to repeated useful routes.
Do not average away organism-specific developmental biographies.

CURRENT EVIDENCE TO SEED
Integrate the current evidence conservatively:
- original Founder Search showed very limited source opportunity under the old motor process;
- integrity analysis produced possible softening/avoidance sequences, but also mixed/adverse cases;
- M1 greatly improved blind world exposure;
- long M1 resurrection histories produced repeated source/integrity experience and increased learned structure;
- useful learned navigation is not established;
- learned E/I motor effects increase with age but remain small relative to M1;
- H broadly enters a dynamic band while H-use/q continue to change;
- E development remains active in some long-lived organisms;
- I is strongly event-driven.
Preserve exact source/report references in the watchlist rather than relying on this summary where possible.
