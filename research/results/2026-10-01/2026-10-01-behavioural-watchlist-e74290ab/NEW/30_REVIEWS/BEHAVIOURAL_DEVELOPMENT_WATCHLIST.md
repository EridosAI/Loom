# Behavioural development watchlist

**Live observational framework · established 1 October 2026 · revision 1.**

What behavioural changes should we watch for when asking whether lived experience is beginning to matter? This document preserves phenomena, ways to observe them, competing explanations and evidence gaps. It is not a success score, pass/fail rubric, definition of intelligence, required developmental sequence, mechanism specification or canonical claim about what Loom must do. Items may be added, revised, split or retired as evidence changes. There is no overall score or ranking of organisms.

## Standing rule: opportunity comes with every observation

**A behavioural improvement is not interpretable without considering whether the organism had comparable opportunity to display that behaviour.** Keep the numerator, denominator, actual observation time, individual identity and event definition together. Report both total exposure and comparisons within comparable contexts: a change in exposure may itself be interesting, so conditioning on exposure must not silently remove the phenomenon being studied.

| Apparent improvement | Opportunity and capability to retain |
|---|---|
| Fewer collisions | Path, hazard approaches, proximity/exposure, mover phase and pre-hazard context |
| More energy contacts | Source exposure, path, source availability and physical approach opportunities |
| More repair | Time damaged, restorative access, eligible contact and remaining capacity to repair |
| More source revisits | Prior encounters, actual departure, elapsed return opportunity and follow-up censoring |
| Softer impacts | Approach speed, angle, geometry, body reserves and actuator capability |
| More avoidance | Comparable pre-hazard situations, including approaches that did not end in collision |

Reduced exposure, immobility, depletion or damage-induced loss of bodily capability must not masquerade as learning. A zero denominator is **NO OPPORTUNITY** for that specific comparison, not a zero success rate. Missing measurements or an unfinished assay are **NOT YET EXAMINED**, not evidence of absence. Lack of contact alone does not prove lack of sensory opportunity. Terminal stops, administrative cutoffs and apparatus interruptions remain distinct; unobserved futures are not failures.

Preserve individual biographies alongside population summaries. Do not pool ordinary Founder Search, externally controlled commissioning, different motor priors and supported resurrection histories as equivalent developmental replicates. Repeated native samples and contact fragments are not independent organisms or independent discoveries. Retain every original fragment; any descriptive regrouping must name its source/version and expose sensitivity. Older integrity and sandbox reports use different grouping definitions; no single threshold is adopted here.

## Evidence labels and record availability

Labels apply to a named phenomenon, organism/interval and source, not to P as a whole. **OBSERVED CHANGE** means a recorded difference; **POSSIBLE DEVELOPMENTAL SIGNAL** means a bounded interpretation worth following; **MIXED** retains opposing patterns; **EVIDENCE AGAINST BENEFICIAL CHANGE** is local to the stated sequence. **CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED** can accompany any observational label. These are descriptive labels, not ordered grades.

Availability statements below distinguish already reported measures, potentially derivable measures needing an explicit assay, and missing causal comparisons. The [runner evidence inventory](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/runner/SCIENTIFIC_EVIDENCE_INVENTORY.md) describes native raw sensory/action/body records, substep physical events, completed-wave diagnostics and full periodic/boundary checkpoints. That is not a claim that every proposed metric has already been calculated. Exact internal operands between checkpoints may require separately scoped reconstruction; none was performed to establish this watchlist.

The later H/E/I curve report deliberately ends RS-M1-008's displayed series at that earlier checkpoint. Its cutoff does not erase the later physical prefix. External support and discarded partial learning packets remain explicit; supported longevity is not ordinary viability. ## BD-01 — Impact / integrity behaviour

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** Fewer collisions at comparable exposure, softer impacts, earlier turning away, increasing clearance or shorter damaging contact.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** Past integrity consequences may alter later action, reducing harm without merely reducing the ability or opportunity to move.

**3. Observable measure.** Collisions per path and per hazard approach; pre-contact normal closing speed; peak individual impulse; finite sustained contact force; damage per collision and per path; trapped/pinned duration; near-miss clearance; hazard-approach outcomes as contact versus avoidance. Keep instantaneous impulse separate from force over positive contact duration; do not invent finite peak force or contact-area stress for instantaneous impacts. Pinning requires an explicit contact/motion/command definition before calculation.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** Path, hazard-approach counts and exposure time; retain contacts as the denominator for severity and all comparable approaches for avoidance. Stratify speed, orientation, E/I, body capability and moving-hazard phase. One collision cannot supply a repeated-impact softening trend.

**5. Major confounds.** Depletion and asymmetric damage-dependent actuation; altered geometry, approach angle and spontaneous drive; displacement caused by the first collision; contact grouping; survivor/censoring effects. Softer individual peaks can coexist with greater aggregate harm.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Partly already measured in the integrity report: contact timing, impulse, finite force, damage, path and sampled clearance. Exact closing-speed recovery was checked for its selected histories. Do not extend that guarantee to unresolved simultaneous/multiple-contact events in other packages. General approach, near-miss and pinning assays remain to be specified.

**7. Current evidence.** **POSSIBLE DEVELOPMENTAL SIGNAL / CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED:** FS-015 softening and FS-017 possible avoidance. **MIXED:** FS-002 has softer later individual peaks but more later contact/damage; FS-010 has sustained separation with mixed local I effects. **EVIDENCE AGAINST BENEFICIAL CHANGE:** FS-044's repeated repair-surface impacts worsen, within that sequence. Long M1 histories have mixed speed/impulse trends. Sources: [integrity report — exact sequence examples and local omissions](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/integrity/IMPACT_INTEGRITY_DEVELOPMENT_ANALYSIS_v0_1.md), [sandbox findings §5–6](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/sandbox/FINDINGS.md).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether retained I state causes lower collision probability, impulse or damage under comparable physical opportunity. Frozen-receiver omissions establish local output influence, not an altered physical trajectory or accumulated benefit.

## BD-02 — Re-encounter of collision-like circumstances

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** A later response differs when sensory/proprioceptive circumstances resemble those preceding earlier damage.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** A changed response in a comparable available context could connect prior experience with later protective behaviour more specifically than an age trend.

**3. Observable measure.** Observer-only matching based primarily on raw sensory state, proprioception, recent motor history, body-relative motion and E/I; cortical/context state may be an additional, explicitly labelled view. Compare later collision probability, clearance, closing speed, turn direction/magnitude, forward drive and damage. Match on information before the outcome. Privileged object ID, global location and analytic bearing may label outcomes or exposure strata, but must not become organism features. Matching on learned cortical state can remove the change under investigation; keep that view distinct from matching on raw available signals.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** All eligible similar pre-hazard contexts before and after experience, with matched/unmatched counts, available follow-up and collision-free approaches included. Report how narrow matching removes opportunities; no matches is not successful avoidance.

**5. Major confounds.** Sensory aliasing, unobserved state, motor autocorrelation, different hazard motion and actuator capacity, retrospective selection of favourable contexts, overlapping windows and unequal follow-up.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Raw native inputs, commands and body histories support a proposed passive matching assay. Exact cortical histories are sparser or need separately scoped reconstruction. Similarity representation, distance, eligibility and follow-up rules are not yet an accepted metric and have not been computed here.

**7. Current evidence.** **NOT YET EXAMINED** as the specified matched-context comparison; **CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED**. FS-015/FS-017 motivate it, but their geometric/local-receiver comparisons are not this assay. Source: [integrity report — limits and nursery relevance](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/integrity/IMPACT_INTEGRITY_DEVELOPMENT_ANALYSIS_v0_1.md).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether an acquired state, rather than different hidden physical or motor conditions, produces the protective change. This remains a promising scalable measurement proposal, not an accepted test or launch instruction.

## BD-03 — Energy-source interaction

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** Productive contact becomes more frequent, better sustained or more effective after experience.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** The organism may begin converting available source encounters into useful intake, beyond chance brushes.

**3. Observable measure.** Productive bouts per time/path; distinct sources, revisits and same-source returns; time/path to revisit; productive dwell; energy gained per bout, contact second and path; approach-to-productive-contact conversion; brush versus sustained contact; command/motion changes immediately after first transfer. Report gross source intake separately from expenditure and net energy change. A positive transfer need not make the interval net-positive.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** Available source-approach/exposure time and path; source stock and renewal; prior productive encounters and actual departures; actual contact seconds for uptake rate; elapsed follow-up. Separate no prior encounter from an observed unsuccessful return opportunity.

**5. Major confounds.** Blind M1 recurrence, stock depletion/renewal, geometry, contact jitter, body state, external energy support, unequal ages and support-enabled access. More local recontacts need not mean navigation.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Contacts, stocks, transfer, path and time are recorded; reports already provide grouped bouts, intake and durations. Approach conversion, true departures and exposure-normalised trends require a specified observer assay. Do not treat every microscopic contact fragment as a new encounter.

**7. Current evidence.** **OBSERVED CHANGE / MIXED / CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED.** Original FS-034 alone received source energy in the fixed 60-life roster; its next eight E-credit handoffs remained negative. That provides little productive-contact opportunity for developmental comparison, not proof of no sensory exposure. All seven long M1 completers had productive contacts; source frequency rose then fell and first-to-last productivity did not consistently improve. Useful learned navigation remains unestablished. Sources: [Founder Search final report](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/founder60/FOUNDER_SEARCH_60_LIFE_FINAL_REPORT.md), [sandbox findings §§1–4](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/sandbox/FINDINGS.md).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether learned state increases productive approach/contact, effective dwell or return beyond matched motor/world opportunity. Current transfer and temporal ordering do not isolate learned seeking.

## BD-04 — Consequential-region revisiting

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** Later returns preferentially involve regions or contexts that previously yielded useful consequences.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** Recurrence may become organised around consequence history, without assuming an internal representation of location.

**3. Observable measure.** Separate ordinary spatial recurrence, source-region return, restorative-region return and return after a previously productive experience. Record return time/path, actual departure and consequence on return; compare with previously visited but nonproductive contexts using an explicit observational definition.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** Previously encountered regions/contexts, productive versus nonproductive histories, number of real departures and remaining time/path in which a return could occur. No prior productive encounter is **NO OPPORTUNITY** for a post-productive-return comparison.

**5. Major confounds.** Arena boundaries, innate loops, nearby fixtures, source renewal, long residence mislabelled as repeated visits, and chance recurrence. Privileged spatial labels belong to the observer, not the organism's asserted knowledge.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Positions and consequence ledgers support spatial bookkeeping; report summaries already contain same-source recontacts. Consequence-conditioned region matching and the distinction between local recontact and spatial return need explicit analysis; no location-memory measure has been validated.

**7. Current evidence.** **OBSERVED CHANGE / CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED:** source recontacts are recorded, including prolonged RS-M1-007 source-3 interaction around 1,986–3,130 s. **NOT YET EXAMINED:** increasing preference for previously useful regions versus comparable recurrence elsewhere. Source: [sandbox findings §§2–4](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/sandbox/FINDINGS.md).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether consequence-acquired state biases returns and their usefulness. Observer identification of a repeated source ID does not establish memory, choice or goal-directed return.

## BD-05 — Exploration and spatial organisation

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** Changes in coverage, recurrence and route structure over an individual's experience.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** Activity may become better organised, including less global exploration if useful places begin to be revisited. More exploration is not automatically more development.

**3. Observable measure.** Coverage growth, revisit fraction, displacement/path ratio, route diversity, repeated loops, local trapping, occupancy distribution and coverage per effort. Record grid/route definitions and sensitivity; body-centre occupancy is not swept-body coverage.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** Actual time, path, effort, prior coverage and accessible arena exposure, with comparable ages and body capability. Distinguish lifetime new cells from new cells within an age window.

**5. Major confounds.** Finite-area coverage saturation, birth geography, innate motor persistence, depletion, mover displacement, local contact constraints, support and binning. A useful route may increase recurrence.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Path, occupancy, coverage, recurrence and reversal summaries already exist. Route-diversity/loop/trapping definitions require further specification; no new threshold is adopted here.

**7. Current evidence.** **OBSERVED CHANGE**, with learned contribution **CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED**. Founder Search moved substantial path with little reach; reversal cancellation was the leading kinematic limitation, while births were broadly distributed. Matched motor v0.2 showed greater M1 reach/lower recurrence in all three starts without greater aggregate effort or contact. That is an innate-prior comparison, not learned development. Long M1 age trends are mixed. Sources: [exploration audit](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/exploration/NEWBORN_EXPLORATION_DYNAMICS_AUDIT_v0_1.md), [birth audit](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/birth_motor_design/BIRTH_GEOGRAPHY_AUDIT_v0_1.md), [motor screen](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/motor_v02/MOTOR_COMMISSIONING_RESULT_v0_2.md), [sandbox findings §7](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/sandbox/FINDINGS.md).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether acquired state changes useful route organisation under matched opportunity, separating innate motor differences from within-life changes.

## BD-06 — Repair behaviour

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** After damage, restorative access, contact quality or repeated use changes with experience.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** Behaviour could increasingly couple a damaged body to lawful restoration while avoiding further injury.

**3. Observable measure.** Time/path from damage to restorative contact; repair before another damaging event; restorative dwell and gentleness; gross restoration and net integrity recovery; repeated restorative returns; effective coupling at later ages. Preserve intervening damage separately from restoration.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** Damage histories with remaining follow-up, time below full integrity, restorative-region exposure/approaches, eligible contact seconds and restoration headroom. No damage is **NO OPPORTUNITY** for repair-after-damage, not failure to repair. Competing new damage and interrupted follow-up stay visible.

**5. Major confounds.** Chance contact, weak actuation, surface pinning, restorative geometry, contact-induced damage and overlapping restorative contacts. External reserve restoration is not lawful repair. Do not double-count coincident repair across multiple surfaces.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Event ledgers supply damage/restoration/contact order; body/geometry records support opportunity descriptions. The denominator for comparable damaged-state restorative access and age-dependent coupling has not been completed as a watchlist assay.

**7. Current evidence.** **MIXED / CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED.** Four of seven long M1 completers received some repair; three did not. That difference alone says nothing about matched access or acquired seeking. FS-017's tiny repair and FS-044's adverse contact sequence remain visible. Sources: [sandbox findings §5–6](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/sandbox/FINDINGS.md), [integrity report](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/integrity/IMPACT_INTEGRITY_DEVELOPMENT_ANALYSIS_v0_1.md), [passive methods — repair accounting](../90_SOURCES/behavioural_watchlist_2026-10-01_e74290ab/sandbox/PASSIVE_METHODS.md).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether learning causes restorative approach, gentler coupling or greater net recovery, rather than simply encountering a repair surface.

## BD-07 — Motor organisation / efficiency

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** Experience-dependent departure from the supplied motor prior, with changed command organisation and useful physical effects.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** Learned regulation could reduce wasteful cancellation or improve coupling, while leaving innate movement distinguishable from acquired influence.

**3. Observable measure.** Useful displacement per effort; forward/reverse cancellation, reversal sequences, curvature, surface pinning, command common/differential structure and departures from the innate M1 pattern. Define the physical consequence meant by “useful”; displacement alone is not benefit. Keep current, attenuation, direct feedback and associative evocation distinct.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** Active time, total effort/path, body capability and comparable sensory/contact contexts within the same motor configuration. Preserve the realised M1 input and experience history; neither motor draw nor age is interchangeable across individuals.

**5. Major confounds.** Different innate priors, correlated random drive, energy-dependent actuation, damage, saturation/attenuation, contact displacement and support. Reduced activity may raise a ratio without improving physical benefit.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Native command/kinematic records and stored wave motor components support descriptive summaries. Existing curve analyses calculate immediate learned-bank receiver effects; these are not alternative movements or a complete decomposition of all indirect learning paths.

**7. Current evidence.** **OBSERVED CHANGE** in the M1 versus CURRENT motor screen, classified as innate organisation. **OBSERVED CHANGE / CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED** in learned expression: the curve report's separate late E effects are about 0.22–1.15% of M1 RMS and I effects about 0.005–0.138%; both increase early-to-late across the seven completers but remain small. M1 is not canonical and useful behavioural benefit is not established. Sources: [motor screen](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/motor_v02/MOTOR_COMMISSIONING_RESULT_v0_2.md), [curve report — structural growth versus functional expression](../90_SOURCES/behavioural_watchlist_2026-10-01_e74290ab/curves/H_E_I_DEVELOPMENTAL_CURVES_v0_1.md).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether those acquired terms materially improve physical behaviour with the same prior and initial opportunity. A small instantaneous term neither proves negligible cumulative effect nor demonstrates useful control.

## BD-08 — Sensorimotor contingency

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** Repeated similar available sensory states increasingly accompany systematic action changes.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** Acquired mappings may begin relating sensory context to consequential action rather than only modulating body-state regulation.

**3. Observable measure.** Within-organism early/late matched-context action distributions: chemical imbalance followed by turn; increasing chemical magnitude followed by altered locomotion; impending-contact-like history followed by slowing/turning; post-impact context followed by later avoidance. Retain reaction lag, command direction and physical outcome. Do not infer forward motion solely from heading.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** Repeated qualifying sensory histories, their amplitude/range and body-state distribution, available action/follow-up intervals, and comparable motor-history strata. Unequal chemistry exposure is not equal opportunity.

**5. Major confounds.** Innate feedback, motor-driven sensory change, common body-state causes, M1 autocorrelation, sensor aliasing, retrospective lag selection and privileged-feature leakage.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Raw endpoint sensory channels and delivered actions are retained, with step-start input supplied by the preceding endpoint/initial envelope. Matched contingency definitions and timing must be specified before analysis. The broader curve-package analysis protocol contains proposals; its presence is not evidence that this behavioural assay is complete.

**7. Current evidence.** **NOT YET EXAMINED** as a completed matched-context developmental assay in the sources cited here; **CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED**. Nonzero learned outputs and H/q changes alone do not establish a useful chemical-turn mapping. Sources: [evidence inventory](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/runner/SCIENTIFIC_EVIDENCE_INVENTORY.md), [curve report — scope boundary](../90_SOURCES/behavioural_watchlist_2026-10-01_e74290ab/curves/H_E_I_DEVELOPMENTAL_CURVES_v0_1.md).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether acquired sensory-dependent control, rather than innate feedback or movement creating the cue, produces the action and improves its consequence.

## BD-09 — Behavioural consistency after experience

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** Responses to comparable consequential contexts become more repeatable after experience.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** Experience might stabilise a useful response; reduced variability itself is not beneficial.

**3. Observable measure.** Within-context spread and directional consistency of source-like approach, impact-like avoidance and low-E motor modulation; relate each to intake, harm or another named physical consequence. Retain distributions and adverse outcomes rather than only means.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** Repeated matched contexts per individual and age period, comparable available action range, exposure and follow-up. Report effective independent encounters separately from overlapping/autocorrelated samples.

**5. Major confounds.** Stereotyped innate activity, body weakening, clipping/saturation, pinning, narrower sensory exposure, support resets and selected favourable intervals.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Stored sensory/action/body histories could support the descriptive comparison. No completed within-context consistency assay is registered here; neither a bank norm nor lower command variance substitutes for it.

**7. Current evidence.** **NOT YET EXAMINED / CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED.** Existing reports contain event sequences and expression scales, not established experience-dependent beneficial consistency. Sources: [sandbox developmental questions §§8, 11](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/sandbox/DEVELOPMENTAL_QUESTIONS.md), [curve report](../90_SOURCES/behavioural_watchlist_2026-10-01_e74290ab/curves/H_E_I_DEVELOPMENTAL_CURVES_v0_1.md).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether acquired state stabilises physically useful responses, rather than constraining the organism into repeatable ineffective behaviour.

## BD-10 — Individual developmental phase changes

**1. Phenomenon to watch.** Rare transitions: first sustained source revisiting, sudden impact softening, long productive dwell, collision-to-avoidance change, repair following damage, or broad roaming giving way to useful repeated routes.

**2. Why it could indicate development.** Important changes may occur within one biography and disappear in population averages.

**3. Observable measure.** Dated event timelines with preceding experience, internal changes, later behaviour, persistence and reversals. Preserve the first observation and subsequent opportunities, including failures to repeat it. Any change-point rule is a proposed analysis with declared eligibility, not a success gate.

**4. Opportunity denominator.** Each organism's observed age/exposure, number of relevant encounters before/after the candidate transition and remaining follow-up. Retain all twelve sandbox individuals and all sixty Founder Search entries, while distinguishing their unequal observation boundaries.

**5. Major confounds.** Chance first events, regression after extremes, many possible transition searches, external support, resource renewal, motor-state changes and missing interrupted tails. Do not select founders from interesting biographies by implication.

**6. Can current records measure it?** Event histories, wave series and checkpoint changes support a chronological account. Exact times of between-checkpoint matrix changes may be unresolved; no interpolation or missing-tail reconstruction is licensed. A formal behavioural phase-change assay has not been completed here.

**7. Current evidence.** **POSSIBLE DEVELOPMENTAL SIGNAL / CAUSAL EFFECT UNRESOLVED:** FS-015/FS-017 and the sandbox's ordered consequence → internal change → later useful contact sequences. RS-M1-002's later longer source contact is one example, not proof of a learned phase transition. **MIXED:** later productive dwell and harm do not improve monotonically. Sources: [integrity report](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/integrity/IMPACT_INTEGRITY_DEVELOPMENT_ANALYSIS_v0_1.md), [sandbox findings §11](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/sandbox/FINDINGS.md), [exact temporal state differences](../90_SOURCES/developmental_catchup_2026-10-01_7c219ab4/sandbox/TEMPORAL_STATE_DELTAS.json).

**8. What would require causal testing.** Whether a persistent acquired change causes the transition and its useful consequences. Temporal ordering is necessary to some hypotheses but is never sufficient evidence of causation.
